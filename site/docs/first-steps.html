Guide

First steps

Your Box is installed. Now: find it, pair your browser, deploy something, and save your recovery kit. No account. And if you used the Configurator, no SSH.

Find your Box

A Box advertises itself on the LAN. Reach its dashboard at:

http://<hostname>.local:2693

The dashboard listens on port 2693. If .local (mDNS) doesn't resolve on your network, use the Box's IP from your router's lease table.

Pair your browser

Management answers to you. Every action needs a session, and a session comes from a one-time pairing code. The coarse health of a Box is public (that's how discovery works), but nothing else is.

  1. Open http://<box>.local:2693 and you're sent to Pair.
  2. Enter the pairing code from your recovery kit (the Configurator generated it, and first boot installed its hash). Your browser gets a session; the code is now spent.

Didn't use the Configurator? SSH in with your key and mint a code:

ssh <you>@<box>.local
boxd auth enroll            # prints a code, valid 15 min, single use
You can reach the console over an SSH forward (ssh -L 2693:localhost:2693 <box>) from anywhere, but it still asks you to pair: reaching the port is not proof of ownership. Every service the Box runs can also reach 127.0.0.1:2693, so if that were authority, anything you deployed could take the console over. Pair once with a code and the browser keeps its session.

The dashboard

Everything is here, and every change is an atomic generation you can roll back:

SectionWhat it does
ServicesAdd and remove services from templates. Each change builds a new generation.
GenerationsThe history. Roll back to any previous generation in one click.
SystemWhat platform you're running, and updates.
BackupConfigure, run, and restore backups.
NetworkPublic access via your own Cloudflare tunnel.
FleetOther Boxes on the network, and a link into each one's dashboard.
DevicesYour paired browsers and agents: add or revoke them.

Deploy your first service

Go to Services → New, pick a template, fill in its options, and apply. The Box generates the config, builds a new generation, switches to it, and health-checks, rolling back automatically if it doesn't come up. There's a matching JSON/MCP API, so an agent can do the same.

Your recovery kit

Keep these somewhere safe (a password manager). They're generated in your browser and never sent to anyone, which also means only you have them:

  • Pairing code: pairs your first browser, no SSH needed.
  • SSH private key, if the Configurator generated one for you.
  • Backup key: shown once when you turn on backups. Without it, backups can't be restored, by you or anyone.
