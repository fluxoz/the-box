Guide

Remote access

Two ways to reach a Box that isn't on your LAN: Box Connect for private access over an encrypted mesh (only you), and a Cloudflare tunnel for making a service public. Neither one opens a port.

Box Connect: private mesh

Box Connect joins your Box to a WireGuard mesh (via Tailscale/Headscale). You get mutually-authenticated, encrypted L3 reachability to any service on the Box, not only the dashboard, from anywhere, with NAT traversal and relay fallback. Nothing is exposed to the public internet.

Free: your own coordinator

Run your own Headscale, mint a pre-auth key, and join:

boxd connect enroll --server https://your-headscale --authkey <key>
boxd connect status        # show the mesh
boxd connect down          # leave

Managed: we run the coordinator

Don't want to run Headscale + relays? The managed tier provisions a mesh key and joins in one step:

boxd cloud connect

It mints an ephemeral, tagged key from the control plane and joins. Box Connect rides on the same operator identity as pairing. The mesh is yours.

Public access: Cloudflare tunnel

To make a service reachable to anyone (a public site), bring your own Cloudflare tunnel: paste a tunnel token on the dashboard's Network page (or in the Configurator at install), and point the tunnel's hostname at localhost:2693. The Box comes online reachable from the internet with no open inbound ports, and no traffic through us.

Private vs public. Box Connect is you reaching your Box privately. A Cloudflare tunnel is the world reaching a service you choose to publish. Use whichever fits.
