We don't know if AGI is coming.
We know rent is.

Every year, more of your work passes through a model. Code, email, contracts, questions you would not ask out loud. That part is settled. The open questions are quieter: whose machine it runs on, who can read the logs, and what it costs once they know you can't leave.

Intelligence is becoming infrastructure. Infrastructure has landlords.

Renting compute for your code and renting intelligence for your work are the same landlord twice. The frontier models are astonishing, and you will keep renting some of them. That part is fine. The problem is everything else moving into the same buildings: your notes, your codebase, your assistant's memory of your life. When the meter is theirs, the price is theirs. When the logs are theirs, so is the training data. Nobody who owned the pipe has ever declined to raise the rate.

The hedge is not beating them. It's needing them less.

A 128 GB machine on a desk now runs gpt-oss-120b at conversation speed. It is not the smartest model on earth and it does not need to be. It covers the daily eighty percent, holds the context you would never upload, works when the internet doesn't, and answers to nobody. The frontier becomes something you choose for the hard problems, not the landlord of your whole working life.

You can't buy a share of the singularity. You can buy 128 GB of unified memory.

What we are not saying

Local models trail the frontier, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something with worse invoices than ours. We are not predicting doom and we are not naming dates. We are noticing two things: owning your tools has never once been the wrong long-term call, and the window where a private machine runs serious intelligence opened about a year ago.

The software here is free forever either way. Install it on anything with a spare hundred gigabytes and a UEFI. The store is for people who want the machine to show up working, with the price of the hardware shown at cost and a tokens-per-second guarantee in writing.
