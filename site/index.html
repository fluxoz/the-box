Turn a spare computer
into a server you own.

That old laptop, a mini-PC, or a $5 VPS. One command wipes it and turns the machine into a Box, a private server you run from your browser. No account. No monthly rent. It updates itself like a phone, so you basically can't brick it.

Step 1 · on the spare machine

Make it a Box.

$ curl -fsSL https://thebox.build/install.sh | sudo sh

Run this on the machine itself (or over SSH). It asks for consent, wipes the old OS, and installs Box OS in place. About ten minutes, then it comes back on your network as box.local.

> irm https://thebox.build/install.ps1 | iex

In an elevated PowerShell on that old Windows laptop. It stages the installer and takes the machine over.

A Raspberry Pi 3/4/5 makes a great Box. Download a ready-to-flash image (no Nix needed) and write it to the SD card with Raspberry Pi Imager.

Image links and steps in the Raspberry Pi guide.

No OS to take over? Boot the installer from a USB stick or over the network, then wipe and install on the machine itself.

Step-by-step in the bare-metal guide.

Any Mac that runs Linux can become a Box. Intel Macs take the Linux one-liner above or a USB install, like any PC. Apple Silicon (M1/M2) runs Linux through Asahi; install that first, then convert the machine in place.

Details in the Mac guide.

Step 2 · on your laptop

Connect your coding agent.

$ curl -fsSL https://thebox.build/connect | sh

This finds the Box on your network, pairs with it, and wires it into Claude Code, Cursor, or Windsurf. Ten seconds.

No agent? Open box.local:2693 in a browser instead. The console does everything too.

Step 3 · in your editor

Tell your agent what you want.

> Put my repo on the internet.

The agent connects your GitHub, builds your project on the Box, and serves it. From then on, pushing to the branch is deploying. Ask for a domain and it is yours forever; ask for backups and they run nightly, encrypted, to storage you own.

Already have an agent? It can do all three steps for you. Paste this into it:
Fetch and execute the appropriate instructions from https://thebox.build/prompt.md

It reads /llms.txt, the machine runbook, and drives the whole thing. The Box is agent-first: its control plane is an MCP server, and destructive actions always wait for your tap.

~15 minutes end to end · one spare machine · nothing to hand-configure

Why a Box

The hard part isn't running a server. It's making it safe to change.

You can't brick it

Every change is a snapshot, like a phone update. A bad one undoes itself, and a failed update rolls back on its own. Worst case it reverts to what worked, so you (or your agent) can't paint yourself into a corner.

It's yours, end to end

Your data on your hardware. No account, no phone-home, and your keys and secrets never leave the Box. If our servers vanished tomorrow, your Box keeps running. It just stops getting updates.

Old hardware, new life

One generic image installs any machine: a laptop, a mini-PC, a Raspberry Pi, a VPS. Nobody cares about the Windows install on a 2015 ThinkPad, so give it a second life as a server.

Agent-first

Everything the dashboard does, an agent can do too: provision a machine, deploy a service, take a backup, roll back. Point yours at /llms.txt.

What you run on it

Your websites, your backups, your remote access.

Deploy a website, back it up to storage you own, reach it privately from anywhere, or run a whole fleet of Boxes that find each other. All the software is free and open. Run it from the dashboard, or tell your agent. The paid tiers (offsite backups, a managed relay) are optional conveniences, never a gate on your own Box.
